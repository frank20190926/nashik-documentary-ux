The hard part isn’t the screens. It’s the step between them.

“The problem to solve is that how the user transitions between the video and text and other ways of consuming information.”— contest brief
  1. Every excursion has a return pathReading, mapping or saving never strands the viewer. One tap puts them back in the film.
  2. One foreground stream at a timeWhen text takes over, the video docks to 72 px and pauses. When the film resumes, the text steps aside.
  3. Re-entry restores the thought, not just the timecodeResume rewinds 5 s to the start of the narrator’s sentence.
§ 01

The 3-Speed Attention Model

One film, three ways to watch it. A viewer shifts between them several times in 15 minutes, so the design offers three transition costs: zero, one tap, and self-paced. Pick a mode to see its wireframe, steps and cost.

The three modes, side by side

Same documentary, same content. What differs is how much the film is interrupted and what it costs to come back.

§ 02

Interactive 5-State UX Wireframe Flow

The whole transition system is a small state machine: six screens (State 3 splits into 3A and 3B) and two return loops. Click any state, or step through a path, to see its wireframe, gesture, system response, audio/video behaviour and edge-case rules.

    § 03

    Information Architecture: 6 Eras × 4 Tabs × 3 Reading Depths

    The reference screen already contains the whole system. This section places every element of it into one structure, so each piece of content has exactly one home and one way back to the film.

    6

    Eras form the spine. The rail and the film chapters are the same thing.

    3

    Evidence types: Inscriptions, Coins, Cave / architectural remains.

    3

    Drawers: Places, People, Primary sources.

    4

    Tabs: Timeline, Map, Stories, Saved, each with 3 reading depths.
